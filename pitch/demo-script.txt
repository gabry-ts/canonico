Q&A PREP (about 1:30, answer in two sentences, then stop)

1. Does it connect to our systems?
   Yes: document uploads, a versioned document library, public GitHub repositories, a
   mailbox, and webhooks for meetings, notes and tickets. We have not run it on a real
   customer's data yet.

2. Does it scale? Can we have several teams or projects?
   One organisation holds isolated projects, each with its own sources, members and roles.
   Today it is one process with state in a file on one server; for a pilot we move state to
   a database and add single sign-on, since the demo has no real login.

3. Does it work in Italian?
   Yes. The interface and the answers are in English and Italian.

4. How do you deploy?
   Docker on your own server, one command, then a four-step setup wizard in the browser.
   The demo runs on ours.

5. Why not Mem0 or Zep?
   Mem0's model decides by itself what to add, and its open-source extraction only adds, so
   old facts pile up [verified: KVA track research]. Zep keeps fact history but has no screen
   where an operations lead retires a fact; that screen is what we built.

6. Why not OIDA itself?
   OIDA is KVA's benchmark and the paper that says retrieval is not enough. We used its
   data to test; Canonico is the tool a process owner uses to see and fix the binding fact.

7. Why not Glean or Copilot?
   They find documents and answer from them; deciding which version still binds is left to
   the reader [unverified: we did not test them today]. Canonico can sit under them through
   its API and hand them the binding fact with its source.

8. GDPR: can you erase personal data?
   Not yet. Retire keeps the history, which is right for a superseded procedure. Real erasure
   under Article 17 is the next thing we build.

9. What else is in the product?
   A dashboard of stale facts, a maintenance queue that proposes what to retire, an audit
   log with undo for every action. Built today: a FastAPI backend with 112 API operations
   and 107 tests, and a React frontend.

10. Isn't five queries too few?
    Yes, it is a first signal. Three of five against zero of five on KVA's own corpus, with
    the model routing the topic (two of five offline). Next step is the full ClearPath set.

11. Who pays, and how much?
    The AI programme lead of a company rolling out an internal assistant, with process owners
    and compliance in the room. Six thousand euros for an eight-week pilot, then fifteen
    thousand a year; CAC 9,300, payback 11.2 months. Our estimates [declared].

12. Why this team?
    Emanuele comes from banking and finance, where which decision binds is a regulatory
    question every day. Gabriele built the backend and the benchmark, Davide the pitch and
    the business model.

13. What happens after the hackathon?
    Five people outside the team find and fix a wrong fact alone while we time them, five
    conversations with AI programme leads, then a first paid pilot. The repo stays public.
