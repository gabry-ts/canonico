Q&A PREP (about 1:30, answer in two sentences, then stop)

1. Why not OIDA itself?
   OIDA is KVA's benchmark and the paper that says retrieval is not enough. We used its
   data to test; Canonico is the tool a process owner uses to see and fix the binding fact.

2. Why not Glean or Copilot?
   They find documents and answer from them; deciding which version still binds is left to
   the reader [unverified: we did not test them today]. Canonico can sit under them through
   its API and hand them the binding fact with its source.

3. Why not Mem0 or Zep?
   Mem0's model decides by itself what to add, and its open-source extraction only adds, so
   old facts pile up [verified: KVA track research]. Zep keeps fact history but has no screen
   where an operations lead retires a fact; we are that screen and the rules behind it.

4. Who pays?
   The AI programme lead of a company rolling out an internal assistant. Process owners and
   compliance are in the room because they own the facts [declared].

5. How much?
   A paid pilot of six thousand euros for eight weeks, then fifteen thousand a year per
   company. Our estimate, to check with the first buyers [declared].

6. What happens with real company data?
   The connectors are there: document uploads, a versioned library, GitHub, mail and webhooks
   for meetings and tickets. We have not run it on a real customer's data, and the demo has no
   real login yet; single sign-on comes before any pilot.

7. GDPR: is retire the same as erasure?
   No. Retire keeps the history, which is right for a superseded procedure. Personal data
   under Article 17 must leave the memory and the audit must not keep its content. That is
   not built yet and is the next feature for the compliance buyer.

8. Isn't five queries too few?
   Yes, it is a first signal. Three of five against zero of five, on KVA's own corpus, with the
   model picking the topic (two of five offline), and we know why the two misses fail. Next step is the full ClearPath set.

9. How does it scale?
   Today it is one process with state in a file, one replica on our server, and every
   organisation has isolated projects. For a pilot we move state to a database. Model cost is
   small: about forty cents of model calls so far for the whole build and corpus.

10. Why this team?
    Emanuele comes from banking and finance, where which decision binds is a regulatory
    question every day. Together we built a backend with 112 API operations, 107 tests
    and the full interface during the event.

11. What happens after the hackathon?
    Timed test with five outside people, five conversations with AI programme leads, then a
    first paid pilot on one process. The repo stays public.
