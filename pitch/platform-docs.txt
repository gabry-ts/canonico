Track: Making memory visible to people

A new hire asks the company assistant how long onboarding takes. It says four weeks, a figure the final report of 28 Nov 2025 replaced with three to four weeks.

Live: Giulia, the operations lead, sees the binding fact with its verbatim source, the four versions it replaced and the stale "4 weeks" still active. She retires it and the next answer is "three to four weeks". No ticket, no engineer.

First test, 5 evolving-decision questions from KVA's ClearPath corpus: binding version right 3/5 (model router), plain retrieval 0/5.

Demo: [DEMO_URL]
Code: https://github.com/gabry-ts/canonico

Team
Emanuele Beffa (banking and finance background, frontend), Davide Chiattelli (pitch and business model), Gabriele Partiti (backend, deploy, benchmark).

Plan
Canonico keeps an organisation's memory split into isolated projects, each fed by sources: documents, a versioned document library, public GitHub, mail and webhooks for meetings, notes and tickets. It extracts facts with their verbatim quote, links each fact to the one it replaces and says which decision is binding now. People find, correct, retire or pin a fact, and the assistant uses the change in its next answer. Every action is audited and can be undone.

Riskiest assumption [declared]
The AI programme lead of a company rolling out an internal assistant will pay for a separate layer that decides which fact is binding, instead of waiting for the assistant vendor's memory settings.

Kill criterion [declared]
If three of the first five AI programme leads we talk to say their vendor's memory already lets a process owner retire a stale fact, we stop selling the layer and look at the compliance buyer instead.
