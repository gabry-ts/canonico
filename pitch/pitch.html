Canonico

The company assistant knows which decision is binding now, and the people who own the decision can fix it.

01Monday morning

A new hire asks the company assistant how long onboarding takes. It says four weeks. The final report of 28 November 2025 changed that to three to four weeks, after four older versions. Today the only way to find out why the assistant is wrong is a ticket to an engineer.

02What Canonico does

Giulia runs operations. She asks the same question in Canonico and sees the binding answer, three to four weeks, with the sentence from the final report it comes from. Under it she sees the four versions it replaced and the old "4 weeks" that is still active. She retires it. The assistant's next answer is "three to four weeks". She did it in the browser, without a ticket.

Live demo Code on GitHub

03How it works

  1. A company connects its sources to a project: documents, a versioned document library, public GitHub, mail, and webhooks for meetings, notes and tickets.
  2. Canonico pulls out each decision with its verbatim quote and links it to the one it replaces, so it can say which version binds today.
  3. People find, correct, retire or pin a fact. The next answer uses the change. Every action is in the audit log and can be undone.

04The one number

3 of 5 against 0 of 5

We asked five questions about decisions that changed over time, on ClearPath, the 46-document corpus from KVA's own OIDA benchmark. Canonico gave the binding version three times. Plain retrieval gave it zero times. It is a first test on five queries, so read it as a signal.

05Why now

Companies are putting assistants with memory in front of their staff. The memory tools on the market are built for developers or for one person's chat. KVA's own research for this track finds no organisation-wide place to see what the system believes, with source and history, and fix it so the next answer changes.

06Competition

Mem0, Zep and Letta store memory for developers; Zep keeps fact history but has no screen for a process owner. Claude and ChatGPT let one user edit their own memory, without sources or versions.

Among today's teams, others track an old and a new supplier file or replay credit decisions under rules. Canonico is the layer underneath: which version binds, across any source, fixed by the person who owns it.

07Business model

We sell to the AI programme lead of a company rolling out an internal assistant, with process owners and compliance at the table. A paid eight-week pilot on one process costs €6,000, then an annual licence of €15,000. Our year-two assumption is 12 pilots and 8 licences, about €192,000 of revenue. Acquisition costs about €14,000 per licensed customer, which pays back in about 12 months, or about 4 net of pilot fees. All of these are our estimates, still to check with buyers.

08What's next

Time five people outside the team as they find and fix a wrong fact alone. Add real erasure for personal data, since retiring keeps history. Test on the full ClearPath set. Then a first paid pilot.
