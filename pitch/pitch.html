Canonico

Giulia runs operations. She knows onboarding takes three to four weeks, and the company AI keeps telling new hires four.

01Monday morning

A new hire asks how long onboarding takes. The AI says four weeks. The final report of 28 November 2025 says three to four. To fix it today, Giulia opens a ticket and waits for an engineer.

02What Canonico does

It shows what the AI believes, where it came from and what it replaced, and lets the person who knows the facts retire the wrong one. The next answer changes.

03Live, two minutes

  1. We ask "How long does onboarding take?" The AI says four weeks. Wrong.
  2. We open what the AI believes. Three to four weeks is binding, quoted from the final report. Under it sit the four versions it replaced, and the old "4 weeks" still active.
  3. Giulia retires "4 weeks" and writes why.
  4. We ask again. Three to four weeks, and nobody opened a ticket.

When a client email later sets a new target, Canonico reads it and updates the memory itself.

04The one number

3 of 5 against 0 of 5

We asked five questions about decisions that changed over time, on KVA's ClearPath corpus. Canonico found the binding version three times, with a model routing each question to its topic. Plain retrieval found it zero times. It is a first test on five queries.
